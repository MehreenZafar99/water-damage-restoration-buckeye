If your ceiling is dripping or sagging

  • Move people and pets out of the room. A ceiling full of water can collapse.
  • Move furniture and electronics out from under the leak.
  • Put a bucket under drips. If the ceiling is bulging, a small hole at the lowest point can let water drain in a controlled way. Only do this if you can stand safely, and stay out from under it.
  • Turn off lights and fans in that room if water is near fixtures.

How roofs leak in Buckeye

Concrete tile roofs

Many Buckeye homes have concrete tile roofs. The tiles themselves shed water well, but the real waterproofing is the underlayment beneath them. Years of intense sun dry out and crack that underlayment. The tiles still look fine from the street, but water gets through during a heavy storm.

Flat roofs and patio covers

Flat and low slope sections, common on older homes and patio covers, can pond water. Aging coatings crack in the heat and let water through.

Cracked or slipped tiles

Monsoon winds can lift, crack or shift tiles, opening a direct path for rain.

Flashing and vents

Water often gets in around roof vents, chimneys, skylights and where roofs meet walls.

Gutters and scuppers

Clogged gutters and scuppers overflow during heavy rain and can push water back under the roof edge.

Ceiling leaks that are not from the roof

Not every ceiling stain means a roof problem. In Buckeye homes, ceiling water damage also comes from:

  • AC condensate overflow from an attic air handler. See AC leak water damage.
  • Plumbing lines in the attic
  • A bathroom or laundry room on the floor above in two story homes
  • Attic water heaters

We find the source as part of every inspection.

Why ceiling water damage gets worse quickly

Water on top of a ceiling does not just drip through. It soaks into the attic insulation, which holds it against the drywall like a wet sponge. The drywall softens, sags and can fall. Meanwhile, the attic heat in a Buckeye summer creates perfect conditions for mold to grow on wet wood and paper faced drywall.

How we repair ceiling and roof leak damage

  1. Inspect from below and above. We check the ceiling with moisture meters and thermal cameras and look in the attic to see how far the water spread.
  2. Remove wet insulation that cannot be dried.
  3. Remove or dry ceiling drywall. Sagging or badly soaked drywall is cut out. Lightly wet areas can sometimes be dried in place.
  4. Dry the attic framing and the room below.
  5. Check for mold on rafters, sheathing and the back of drywall.
  6. Repair the ceiling. New drywall, matching texture, primer and paint.

The roof itself needs to be repaired by a roofer before the ceiling is closed up. We can take photos of the attic and damage to help with the roof repair and insurance claim. If needed, a temporary tarp can protect the home until the roofer arrives.

What it usually costs

A single stained ceiling panel is a smaller job. A long running leak with wet insulation across several rooms costs more. Most water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500. We give you a written estimate after inspecting.

Insurance and roof leaks

Water that comes in because of sudden storm damage, like tiles blown off by monsoon wind, is often covered. Leaks from a worn out roof or long term wear may not be. Photos of the roof and the attic taken right away make a big difference. See our insurance claim guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can a water stained ceiling be painted over?

Only after the leak is fixed and the drywall and insulation are completely dry. Painting over a wet ceiling traps moisture and the stain will come back.

Why does my ceiling only leak during monsoon storms?

Heavy, wind driven rain finds gaps that normal rain does not. Cracked underlayment under tile roofs is a very common cause in Buckeye.

Is a sagging ceiling dangerous?

Yes. A ceiling holding water can collapse without warning. Keep people out of the room and call right away.

Do you repair roofs?

We repair the water damage inside the home. A roofer fixes the roof. We can document the damage to help both the roofer and your insurance company.

How long does ceiling repair take?

Drying often takes two to four days. Drywall repair, texture and paint usually take a few more days after that.

Will I need new insulation?

Usually, if it got soaked. Wet insulation holds moisture against the ceiling and loses its ability to keep heat out.
