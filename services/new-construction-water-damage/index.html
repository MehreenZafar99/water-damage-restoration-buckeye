New homes leak more often than people think

Buckeye is one of the fastest growing cities in the country, with thousands of new homes built every year. Every one of those homes has hundreds of new plumbing connections, fixtures, roof penetrations and drain lines. Most work perfectly. Some do not, and a failure in the first few years is more common than most buyers expect.

When it happens, the stress is real. You just moved in, and now there is water on your new floors.

Common causes of water damage in new homes

Plumbing connections that were not fully tightened

Supply line nuts, compression fittings and PEX connections can work loose in the first months as the system settles and water pressure cycles.

Nail or screw through a pipe

A drywall screw or trim nail that nicked a pipe can leak slowly for weeks before water shows up.

Shower pans and tub drains

New shower pans that were not sealed or sloped properly can leak into the subfloor or the wall behind.

Fixture and appliance hookups

Dishwasher, fridge and washer connections made during move in are a common source of early leaks.

Roof and window flashing

Missed or loose flashing around windows, doors and roof vents often shows up during the first monsoon season.

Drainage and grading

In newer neighborhoods, yard grading and street drainage are still settling. During heavy rain, water can pool against a new home and come in under doors or along the slab edge.

AC condensate lines

New AC systems sometimes have lines that are not sloped correctly or pans without float switches.

What to do if your new home leaks

  1. Stop the water at the fixture valve or main shutoff.
  2. Take photos and video of the leak, the source and every wet area before anything is moved. This is very important for warranty claims.
  3. Write down the date and time you found it.
  4. Notify your builder in writing, following the steps in your warranty paperwork.
  5. Do not wait too long for someone else to start drying. Most warranties and insurance policies expect you to limit further damage.
  6. Call (623) 555-0148 to get drying started.

Builder warranty and insurance

New homes usually come with a builder warranty that covers certain defects for a set period. Your homeowners insurance may also cover sudden water damage. Which one pays, and for what, depends on the cause and the terms of each.

We do not take sides. Our job is to stop the damage and give you clear, detailed documentation:

  • Photos of the source and all affected areas
  • Moisture readings showing how far the water spread
  • A written scope of the work needed

That record helps whether you are working with a builder, a warranty company, your insurance company or all three.

How we handle new home water damage

  1. Find the source and the full spread with moisture meters and thermal cameras.
  2. Document everything before and during the work.
  3. Extract water and start drying right away.
  4. Save new materials where possible. New drywall, flooring and cabinets often dry well when drying starts early.
  5. Remove what cannot be saved and keep records of what was removed.
  6. Repair and restore once the source has been fixed.

Why quick action matters even more in a new home

New homes are often sealed tightly for energy efficiency. That is great for your power bill but it traps moisture inside walls. New drywall and wood framing also carry some leftover construction moisture. Add a leak, and drying needs to start fast to keep mold from getting a foothold.

What it usually costs

Costs depend on the source and how far water spread. Most water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500. We give you a written estimate you can share with your builder or insurance company.

Frequently asked questions

Will my builder warranty cover water damage?

It depends on the cause and the warranty terms. Construction defects are often covered for a set period. Read your warranty paperwork and report the issue in writing right away.

Should I wait for the builder before drying?

Notify the builder right away, but do not let the water sit. Most warranties and policies expect you to limit further damage. Document everything first, then start drying.

Can you work with my builder?

Yes. We provide photos, moisture readings and a written scope that you can share with your builder or warranty company.

Why would a new home leak?

Loose fittings, nicked pipes, shower pans, appliance hookups and flashing are the most common causes in the first few years.

Is new drywall easier to dry?

Often, yes, if drying starts early and the water was clean. It can usually be dried in place instead of replaced.

Does homeowners insurance cover new home leaks?

Sudden leaks are often covered, though the insurance company may look to the builder if a defect caused it. Good documentation helps either way.
