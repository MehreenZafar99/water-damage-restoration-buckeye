Every floor reacts to water differently

Buckeye homes have a mix of flooring: tile in living areas and kitchens, carpet in bedrooms, and more and more luxury vinyl and laminate in newer builds. Each one handles water in its own way, so each one needs a different plan.

Carpet and pad

Carpet itself is fairly tough. The pad underneath is the real problem. Pad soaks up water like a sponge and holds it against the slab or subfloor.

Often saved: carpet wet with clean water, like a supply line leak, if drying starts within about a day. We extract the water, lift the carpet, and dry both sides. The pad is sometimes replaced even when the carpet is kept, since new pad is inexpensive.

Usually replaced: carpet and pad soaked with floodwater, toilet bowl water or sewage, or clean water that sat too long and started to smell.

Tile over concrete

Tile is the most common floor in Buckeye homes. It looks waterproof, but grout lines are not. Water seeps down through grout and spreads between the tile and the slab. It can then wick into baseboards and the bottom of walls.

Often saved: tile that is still firmly bonded. We dry the grout and slab surface with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Sometimes replaced: tile that sounds hollow, lifts or cracks, or tile sitting over a slab leak that needs to be accessed.

Luxury vinyl and laminate

These floors are popular in newer Buckeye homes. Many vinyl planks resist water on top, but water gets under them through the edges and seams. Laminate has a wood fiber core that swells when wet.

Often saved: some vinyl plank floors, if water is removed quickly and the planks are lifted so the floor underneath can dry.

Usually replaced: laminate that has swollen at the seams or edges. Once it swells, it does not go back down.

Hardwood and engineered wood

Wood floors cup, crown and buckle as they absorb water.

Often saved: wood that is dried quickly with specialty drying mats and dehumidifiers. Many floors can be sanded and refinished once dry.

Sometimes replaced: wood that has buckled badly, separated from the subfloor or been wet for a long time.

Why rushing to replace flooring is a mistake

Putting new flooring over a wet slab or subfloor is one of the most costly mistakes after water damage. The moisture has nowhere to go, so the new floor cups, lifts, grows mold underneath or smells within weeks. We always confirm the floor underneath is dry with moisture readings before any new flooring goes in.

How we handle wet floors

  1. Extract water from carpet, pad and hard surfaces.
  2. Identify the water type so we know what can be saved.
  3. Lift, remove or keep flooring based on type and condition.
  4. Dry the slab or subfloor and the base of nearby walls.
  5. Clean and treat to prevent odor and mold.
  6. Reinstall or replace flooring once everything is dry.

What it usually costs

Carpet that can be dried and reinstalled costs much less than replacement. Flooring replacement, especially tile or hardwood across several rooms, is often the biggest part of the bill. Most water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500, with larger flooring replacements above that. We give you a written estimate after inspecting.

Frequently asked questions

Can wet carpet be saved?

Often, if it was clean water and drying starts within about a day. Carpet soaked with floodwater or sewage usually cannot be saved.

Why does the pad get replaced when the carpet does not?

Pad holds much more water than carpet and is hard to dry fully. It is also inexpensive, so replacing it is often the safer choice.

My tile looks fine. Is there still water under it?

Possibly. Water goes down through grout and spreads under the tile. Moisture readings along the grout and nearby baseboards tell us.

Can laminate flooring be dried?

If it has not swollen yet, sometimes. Once laminate swells at the seams, it usually needs to be replaced.

How soon can new flooring go in?

Only after the slab or subfloor reads dry. That usually means a few days of drying first.

Does insurance pay for new flooring?

If the water damage is covered, damaged flooring is usually part of the claim. Matching flooring in connected rooms depends on your policy.
