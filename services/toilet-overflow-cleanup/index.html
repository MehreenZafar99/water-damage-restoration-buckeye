Stop the overflow first

  1. Turn off the toilet supply valve. It is on the wall behind the toilet, near the floor. Turn it clockwise.
  2. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down if water is still running.
  3. Do not flush again.
  4. Keep kids and pets out of the bathroom.
  5. Call (623) 555-0148

Not all toilet water is the same

How we clean up a toilet overflow depends on what kind of water came out.

Clean water

Water from a broken tank supply line or a cracked tank is clean when it starts. It can be dried like any other clean water leak, as long as it is handled quickly.

Gray water

Water from the bowl that has urine but no solids is considered gray water. It carries bacteria and needs cleaning and sanitizing, not just drying.

Black water

Water with feces, or water that backs up from the sewer line, is black water. It is a real health risk. Carpet, pad and porous materials it touches usually need to be removed, and everything else needs to be disinfected.

Clean water that sits for a day or two in warm conditions can also turn into gray water as bacteria grow. In a Buckeye summer, that happens faster.

Where overflow water goes

Toilet overflow does not stay on the bathroom tile. It runs:

  • Under the vanity and into the cabinet base
  • Under the baseboards and into the bottom of the walls
  • Across the threshold into hallway carpet or bedroom flooring
  • Under the toilet itself, into the subfloor around the flange
  • Through the ceiling below, if the bathroom is upstairs

How we clean up toilet overflows

  1. Identify the water type so we use the right cleaning approach.
  2. Extract the water from all affected floors.
  3. Remove contaminated materials like carpet pad, baseboards or drywall that touched gray or black water.
  4. Clean and disinfect floors, walls, cabinets and fixtures with professional products.
  5. Dry the structure including the subfloor around the toilet and the bottom of walls.
  6. Repair and restore baseboards, drywall, flooring and vanity parts.

Why toilets overflow

  • A clog in the toilet trap or drain line
  • A worn fill valve or flapper that will not stop filling
  • A cracked tank or loose supply line
  • A main sewer line blockage, which can make toilets overflow when other fixtures drain

If more than one drain in the house is backing up, the problem is probably in the main sewer line. See our sewage backup cleanup page.

What to expect during cleanup

Most toilet overflow jobs start the same day you call. The first visit covers water removal, cleaning and setting up drying equipment. Air movers and dehumidifiers usually run for two to four days while we check moisture levels. If baseboards, drywall or flooring were removed, repairs follow once everything reads dry.

If the toilet has to be pulled to dry the subfloor or replace a wax ring, we let you know ahead of time. If you have more than one bathroom, we plan the work so you still have a working toilet while drying runs.

What it usually costs

A clean water tank leak caught quickly is a small job. A black water overflow that spread into carpet or through a ceiling costs more because of removal and disinfecting. Most water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500. We give you a written estimate after inspecting.

Frequently asked questions

Can I clean up a toilet overflow myself?

A small amount of clean tank water on tile, yes. If the water came from the bowl, spread past the bathroom or soaked into carpet or walls, professional cleaning and drying is safer.

Can carpet be saved after a toilet overflow?

If it was clean tank water and it is dried quickly, often yes. Carpet soaked with bowl water or sewage usually has to be replaced.

Is toilet water dangerous?

Bowl water and sewage carry bacteria and viruses. Keep children, older adults and pets away and wash hands after any contact.

How do I know if water got under the floor?

Loose or rocking toilets, stained grout near the base and soft spots in the floor are common signs. We check with moisture meters.

Does insurance cover toilet overflows?

Sudden overflows are often covered. Sewer backups may need a specific add on to your policy.

Will the bathroom smell after cleanup?

Not if it is cleaned, disinfected and dried properly. Lingering smells usually mean moisture or contamination was left behind.
