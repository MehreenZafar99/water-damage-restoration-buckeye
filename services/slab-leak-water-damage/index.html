What is a slab leak?

Almost every home in Buckeye is built on a concrete slab foundation. Water supply lines, and in some homes hot water lines, run through or under that slab. A slab leak happens when one of those lines develops a hole or crack. The water has nowhere to go, so it spreads under your flooring and pushes up through the concrete.

Signs of a slab leak

  • A warm or hot spot on the floor, often from a hot water line leak
  • Damp or discolored grout lines
  • Tile that sounds hollow or starts to lift
  • Carpet that feels damp for no clear reason
  • Baseboards that swell or pull away from the wall
  • A musty smell near the floor
  • Your water bill jumps and nothing else changed
  • The sound of running water when every faucet is off
  • Your water meter keeps moving when nothing in the house is using water

If you notice two or more of these, call a leak detection plumber and then call us about the water damage.

Why slab leaks happen in Buckeye

Copper lines in the concrete

In some Buckeye neighborhoods, copper supply lines were run directly through the slab. Copper that touches concrete and soil can wear from the outside over time. Where lines rub against concrete or rebar as the house expands and contracts, small holes can form.

Hard water from the inside

Buckeye's hard water carries a heavy mineral load. Over many years, that can wear on pipes from the inside, especially at bends and joints.

Soil movement during monsoon season

The desert soil around Buckeye dries out for months, then gets soaked during monsoon storms. That shift in moisture makes the soil swell and shrink, which moves the slab slightly and stresses the pipes inside it.

Hot water lines

Hot water lines expand and contract more than cold lines, which is why many slab leaks are on the hot side. That is also why a warm floor spot is one of the clearest warning signs.

Why slab leak damage is hard to dry

A slab leak is not like a spill on top of the floor. The water is coming from below, and it has usually been leaking for days or weeks before anyone notices. By then:

  • The concrete itself is holding moisture
  • Water has spread under tile, laminate or carpet across a wide area
  • Bottom plates and lower drywall may have wicked up water
  • Mold may have started under flooring or behind baseboards

Buckeye's dry air makes this tricky. The top of a floor can feel dry while the slab underneath is still wet. Flooring put back over a wet slab can buckle, grow mold or lift later.

How we handle slab leak water damage

  1. Map the damage. We use moisture meters and thermal cameras to find exactly how far the water spread under the floor and into walls.
  2. Remove what cannot be dried. Some flooring, pad or baseboards may need to come up so the slab can breathe.
  3. Dry the slab. Dehumidifiers and air movers, sometimes with specialty mats or directed airflow, pull moisture out of the concrete.
  4. Dry the walls. Lower drywall and bottom plates are dried or replaced.
  5. Confirm it is dry. We take readings until the slab and walls reach safe levels.
  6. Restore. Flooring, baseboards and drywall are put back once everything is dry.

We focus on the water damage. The leak itself needs to be located and repaired by a plumber, either by fixing the line through the slab or rerouting it through walls or the attic. We can work alongside your plumber so drying and repair happen in the right order.

What slab leak water damage usually costs

Slab leaks often cost more than surface leaks because flooring may need to come up and concrete takes longer to dry. Cost depends on how big the wet area is, what flooring you have and whether mold is present. Most jobs fall in the range of about $2,000 to $7,500, with larger losses above that. We give you a written estimate after inspecting.

Does insurance cover slab leaks?

Many homeowners policies cover the water damage from a sudden slab leak, and some also cover the cost of accessing the pipe. Most do not cover the pipe repair itself. Leaks that went on for a long time can be harder to claim, which is another reason to act on the warning signs quickly. We document the damage for you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I have a slab leak or just a surface leak?

Surface leaks usually have an obvious source, like a sink or toilet. Slab leaks show up as warm spots, damp grout, lifting tile or a rising water bill with no visible source. A leak detection plumber can confirm it.

Can a wet slab dry on its own?

Very slowly, and often not completely under flooring. Trapped moisture can cause mold and flooring failure later. Professional drying brings it down to safe levels.

Will my tile have to come up?

Not always. Some tile floors can be dried in place. Tile that is loose, cracked or sitting over a very wet area may need to be removed. We check before deciding.

How long does slab drying take?

Concrete takes longer than drywall. Many slab leak jobs need about three to seven days of drying, depending on how long the leak ran.

Do you find and fix the leak?

We handle the water damage. A leak detection plumber locates and repairs the pipe. We can work with your plumber so the drying plan fits the repair.

Are slab leaks common in Buckeye?

Yes. Slab foundations, hard water, hot water lines and soil movement during monsoon season all make slab leaks a common problem here.

Can a slab leak cause mold?

Yes. Water trapped under flooring and behind baseboards, combined with Buckeye's heat, is a common setup for hidden mold.
