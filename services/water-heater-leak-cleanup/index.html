If your water heater is leaking right now

  1. Turn off the cold water valve on top of the heater. If you cannot reach it, turn off the main.
  2. Turn off the power or gas. For an electric heater, flip the breaker. For gas, turn the gas valve to off or pilot.
  3. Keep away from hot water. Water from the tank can be scalding.
  4. Do not try to drain the tank onto the floor. Leave that for the plumber.
  5. Take photos of the heater, the leak and the wet areas.
  6. Call (623) 555-0148

Why water heaters fail early in Buckeye

Buckeye has some of the hardest water in Arizona, often somewhere around 15 to 30 grains per gallon. When that water is heated, minerals drop out and settle at the bottom of the tank as sediment and scale.

That buildup causes a chain of problems:

  • The burner or elements have to work harder to heat through the sediment
  • The bottom of the tank overheats and weakens
  • The anode rod that protects the tank wears out faster
  • Valves and fittings get crusted with minerals and start to drip

The result is that many tank water heaters in Buckeye fail years before the age printed on the label. Local plumbers often see tanks give out in the 6 to 9 year range.

Where water heaters sit in Buckeye homes

Most Buckeye homes put the water heater in one of three places, and each one leads to a different kind of damage.

In the garage

The most common spot. When the tank fails, water spreads across the garage floor and often runs under the door into the house. The shared wall with the laundry room, kitchen or hallway can soak up water from the bottom.

In a closet inside the house

Interior closet heaters are more dangerous for damage. A leak soaks the closet floor, then spreads into carpet, hallways and neighboring rooms. Drywall and door frames around the closet swell quickly.

In the attic

Some homes have heaters in the attic. A failure there sends water through the ceiling, soaking insulation and drywall below.

Warning signs before a tank fails

  • Rust colored water from hot taps
  • Rumbling or popping sounds when the heater runs
  • Water pooling around the base of the tank
  • A drip from the temperature and pressure relief valve line
  • Rust or crusty white deposits on fittings
  • The heater is older than about 8 years

How we clean up water heater damage

  1. Extract the water from floors, carpet and the garage.
  2. Check hidden moisture in shared walls, under cabinets and under flooring.
  3. Remove damaged materials like swollen baseboards or soaked carpet pad.
  4. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  5. Clean and treat affected areas to prevent odor and mold.
  6. Repair drywall, baseboards and flooring.

A plumber handles replacing the heater itself. We focus on the water damage and can work with your plumber so the new heater goes in after the area is dry.

What it usually costs

A garage leak caught quickly is often a smaller job. A closet or attic heater that failed overnight can affect several rooms. Most water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500. We give you a written estimate after we inspect.

Insurance and water heater leaks

Most homeowners policies cover sudden water damage from a failed water heater. They usually do not pay for the heater itself. A slow leak that went on for months may be treated differently. We take photos and moisture readings to support your claim.

Frequently asked questions

How much water can a failed water heater release?

A typical tank holds 40 to 50 gallons. If the cold water supply stays on, it can keep pouring out much more until someone shuts the valve.

Why do water heaters wear out so fast in Buckeye?

Hard water. The minerals settle in the tank as sediment, which overheats the bottom and wears out the protective anode rod early.

Can I prevent a water heater leak?

Flushing the tank once a year, checking the anode rod and using a water softener all help. Replacing an older tank before it fails is the surest way.

Is water from a water heater clean?

It starts clean, but it can pick up rust and sediment from the tank. If it sits for a day or more, it can start to grow bacteria.

Do you replace water heaters?

We handle the water damage. Your plumber replaces the heater, and we can plan drying around that.

The heater is in my garage. Do I still need help?

Often, yes. Garage water commonly runs into the house and soaks the bottom of shared walls. We check with moisture meters to make sure.
