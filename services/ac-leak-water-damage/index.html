How an air conditioner causes water damage

An air conditioner does more than cool the air. It also pulls moisture out of it. That moisture drips off the indoor coil into a drain pan, then flows out through a small condensate drain line to the outside or into a drain.

In a Buckeye summer, when the AC runs most of the day and night, that system handles a steady flow of water. As long as everything drains, you never notice it. When something goes wrong, that water ends up in your ceiling, closet or walls.

Common causes of AC water damage

Clogged condensate line

The most common cause. Dust, algae and sludge build up inside the drain line until water backs up and overflows the pan. Summer heat helps algae grow faster.

Cracked or rusted drain pan

Older drain pans can crack or rust through, letting water drip straight onto whatever is below.

Failed float switch

Many systems have a safety switch that shuts the AC off when the pan fills. If it fails or was never installed, the pan simply overflows.

Disconnected or sagging line

Lines in hot attics can soften, sag or pull loose at a fitting.

Frozen coil

Dirty filters or low refrigerant can make the coil freeze. When it thaws, more water than normal floods the pan all at once.

Where AC leaks show up in Buckeye homes

Attic air handlers

Many Buckeye homes have the indoor unit in the attic. An overflow there soaks attic insulation first, then comes through the ceiling. You might see a brown stain, sagging drywall or water dripping from a light fixture or vent.

Hall closet units

Some homes have the air handler in an interior closet. Overflow here soaks the closet floor and spreads into carpet and neighboring walls.

Garage units

Units in garages can leak onto storage and run toward the house.

Signs of an AC leak

  • Water stains on the ceiling near the air handler or vents
  • Dripping from ceiling vents or light fixtures
  • Wet carpet or floors near the AC closet
  • A musty smell when the AC turns on
  • The AC suddenly shuts off (a float switch doing its job)
  • Water coming from the secondary drain line outside, which often drains over a window

That last one is important. In many homes, the backup drain line exits above a window. If water is dripping from it, your main line is clogged and the pan is filling. Call an HVAC technician before it overflows.

Why AC leaks need fast drying

Attic insulation soaks up water like a sponge and holds it against the ceiling drywall. Wet drywall can sag and collapse. A slow AC leak can also run for weeks in an attic before anyone sees a stain, which gives mold time to grow in the heat.

How we fix AC water damage

  1. Find the source and the spread. Thermal cameras show wet areas in ceilings and walls.
  2. Remove wet insulation that cannot be dried.
  3. Dry or remove ceiling drywall depending on how wet it is and whether it is sagging.
  4. Dry walls, carpet and floors below the leak.
  5. Check for mold in the attic and ceiling cavity.
  6. Repair drywall, texture, paint and insulation.

An HVAC technician needs to clear the line or fix the pan. We focus on the water damage and can plan repairs so the problem does not come back into fresh drywall.

What AC leak damage usually costs

A small ceiling stain caught early can be a smaller job. A long running attic leak with wet insulation and sagging drywall costs more. Most water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500. We give you a written estimate after inspecting.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my AC leaking water into the house?

Usually the condensate drain line is clogged, so water backs up and overflows the drain pan. Cracked pans and failed float switches are other common causes.

Is a small ceiling stain from the AC a big deal?

It can be. The stain is often the only visible part of a larger wet area in the insulation above. It is worth checking before the drywall weakens or mold starts.

Should I turn my AC off?

If water is actively leaking, yes, until the drain is cleared. In Buckeye heat, get an HVAC technician out quickly so you are not without cooling for long.

Does insurance cover AC leak damage?

Sudden overflow damage is often covered. Damage from a leak that went on for a long time, or from lack of maintenance, may not be. Check your policy.

Can wet attic insulation be dried?

Sometimes, but soaked insulation is usually replaced. It is inexpensive and it holds moisture against the ceiling.

How can I prevent AC leaks?

Change filters often, have the condensate line flushed each spring before the heavy cooling season and make sure a float switch is installed.
