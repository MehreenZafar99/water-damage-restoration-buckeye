Yes, mold grows in the desert

Buckeye's outdoor air is dry, so a lot of people are surprised to find mold in their home. But mold does not need humid weather. It needs moisture, something to feed on and warmth. A hidden leak inside a wall gives it all three.

Drywall paper, wood framing, carpet pad and cabinet backs are all food for mold. Add the heat inside a Buckeye home or attic in summer, and mold can start growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours.

Common causes of mold in Buckeye homes

  • Slow slab leaks soaking flooring and baseboards for weeks
  • Dishwasher and sink leaks under cabinets
  • AC condensate overflow in attics and closets
  • Roof leaks during monsoon season that wet attic insulation
  • Water heater leaks in closets and garages
  • Water damage that was dried with fans only and never fully dried
  • Homes left closed up during summer trips or seasonal stays, where a small leak runs for weeks

Signs you may have mold

  • A musty, earthy smell, especially when the AC first turns on
  • Black, green, gray or white spots on walls, ceilings or baseboards
  • Peeling paint or bubbling drywall
  • Staining on the ceiling or around vents
  • Warped cabinet bottoms under sinks
  • Allergy type symptoms that get better when you leave the house

If you smell mold but cannot see it, it is often behind a wall, under flooring or in the attic.

Why you should not just spray it

Household sprays can change the color of mold on a surface, but they do not reach the roots growing inside drywall or wood. Scrubbing or sanding mold can also send spores into the air and spread them to other rooms.

Most important, mold always comes back if the moisture is still there. Real remediation means fixing the water problem, removing the mold and drying the area.

How we handle mold remediation

  1. Find the moisture source. We use moisture meters and thermal cameras to locate the leak or wet area feeding the mold.
  2. Set up containment. Plastic barriers and air filtration keep spores from spreading to clean rooms.
  3. Remove moldy materials. Drywall, insulation, carpet pad and baseboards with mold growth are carefully removed and bagged.
  4. Clean what stays. Framing, subfloor and other solid materials are cleaned and treated.
  5. Filter the air. HEPA air scrubbers capture spores during and after the work.
  6. Dry the area to confirm the moisture problem is gone.
  7. Restore. New drywall, insulation, baseboards and paint.

The source of the moisture, like a pipe or roof leak, needs to be repaired so the mold does not return. We can work alongside your plumber, roofer or HVAC technician.

Mold and your health

Mold can bother people with allergies, asthma or breathing problems, and it can be harder on young children, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system. If someone in your home has symptoms you think are related to mold, talk with a doctor. Our job is to remove the mold and the moisture causing it.

What mold remediation usually costs

A small area under a sink costs much less than mold spread through a wall or attic. Price depends on how large the area is, what materials are affected and how much has to be removed and rebuilt. We give you a written estimate after inspecting.

Insurance and mold

Many policies cover mold when it comes from a sudden, covered water event, like a burst pipe. Mold from long term leaks or humidity is often excluded, and some policies cap mold coverage at a set amount. Acting quickly on water damage is the best way to avoid mold and a claim dispute.

Frequently asked questions

How fast does mold grow after water damage?

Mold can start growing on wet materials within about 24 to 48 hours, and faster in warm conditions like a Buckeye summer.

Can I remove mold myself?

Very small surface spots on hard surfaces can be cleaned. Mold in drywall, insulation or a large area should be handled professionally so spores do not spread and the source is found.

Will bleach kill mold?

Bleach can lighten mold on hard surfaces, but it does not reach mold growing inside porous materials like drywall and wood. It also does not fix the moisture problem.

How do I know if mold is behind my wall?

A musty smell, peeling paint, staining or a history of leaks are common clues. Moisture readings and a look inside the wall confirm it.

Will mold come back after remediation?

Not if the moisture source is fixed and the area is properly dried. Mold returns when moisture returns.

Can I stay in my home during mold remediation?

Usually, yes. Containment keeps the work area sealed off. For large jobs or sensitive family members, some people choose to stay elsewhere for a few days.
