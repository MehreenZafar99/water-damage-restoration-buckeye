What to do in the next ten minutes

If you are standing in water right now, these steps matter more than anything else on this page.

  1. Turn off the water main. Look near the front of the house, in the garage or by the water heater.
  2. Turn off power to wet rooms at the breaker, only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  3. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water. It is not built for it and can shock you.
  4. Get people and pets out of any room where the ceiling is sagging.
  5. Take photos of the water and its source.
  6. Call (623) 555-0148

When you need emergency water removal

You need emergency help any time there is water you can see pooling on the floor. That includes:

  • A burst supply line under a sink or behind a toilet
  • A water heater that has split or started pouring from the bottom
  • A washing machine hose that broke while no one was home
  • Monsoon water that came in under a door or through the garage
  • A ceiling that is dripping or bulging with water
  • Sewage coming up from a drain or toilet

Even a thin layer of water across tile is a problem. Tile floors in Buckeye homes look waterproof, but grout lines let water down into the slab surface and under baseboards, where it wicks up into drywall.

Why the first hours matter so much

Water moves fast and it moves down and sideways. In the first hour it soaks carpet pad, pushes under baseboards and starts climbing drywall. By the end of the first day, drywall can be wet a foot or more above the floor, and cabinets, door frames and furniture legs start to swell. After about 24 to 48 hours, mold can begin to grow on wet materials.

The faster water is removed, the more of your home can be dried and saved instead of cut out and replaced. That is the single biggest factor in how much the job costs.

How we remove water

Extraction

We use powerful truck mounted and portable extractors to pull water out of carpet, pad and hard floors. Extraction removes far more water than any fan or towel can, and it is the step that makes drying possible.

Finding hidden water

Once standing water is gone, we use moisture meters and thermal cameras to see where the water went. This is where most of the damage hides: behind baseboards, under cabinets, inside the bottom of walls and under flooring.

Setting up drying

We place air movers and commercial dehumidifiers based on what we found. In some cases we remove baseboards or drill small holes at the bottom of walls so air can reach inside.

Checking progress

We come back to take moisture readings and adjust equipment until every area reaches a safe, dry level.

Emergency water removal for Buckeye homes

Most Buckeye homes are built on concrete slabs with tile or carpet over them. That changes how water behaves. There is no basement for water to drain into, so it spreads across the whole floor instead. A leak in a hall bathroom can reach bedrooms on both sides within an hour.

Garages are another common spot. Many Buckeye water heaters sit in the garage, and when one fails, water runs under the door into the house and soaks the wall shared with the laundry room or kitchen.

Homes and businesses

We handle emergency water removal for houses, townhomes, rental properties, offices, shops and warehouses. For businesses, we focus first on getting water out of work areas so you can reopen as soon as possible.

What it usually costs

Water removal is usually the first part of a larger restoration job. Most complete water damage jobs in Buckeye fall between about $2,000 and $7,500, depending on how much area is wet, what kind of water it is and how long it sat. We give you a written estimate after we inspect.

Insurance and emergency water removal

Sudden water events like a burst pipe or failed appliance are usually covered by homeowners insurance. Your policy also expects you to take reasonable steps to stop more damage, which is exactly what emergency water removal does. We document everything with photos and moisture readings for your claim.

Frequently asked questions

Do you really answer at night?

Yes. Water damage does not wait for business hours, and neither do we. Call (623) 555-0148 any time.

How long does water extraction take?

Extraction itself usually takes a few hours for a typical home. Drying afterward takes longer, often three to five days depending on the materials.

Should I pull up wet carpet myself?

It is better to wait. Wet carpet is heavy and can tear, and in many cases it can be dried and saved. Pulling it up can also make it harder to document for insurance.

Is it safe to stay in my home?

Usually, as long as the power is safe and the ceiling is not sagging. If sewage or floodwater is involved, keep children, older adults and pets away from affected areas.

What if I cannot find where the water is coming from?

Turn off the main and call us. Finding the source is part of every inspection.

Will my floors need to be replaced?

Not always. Tile over concrete often only needs the grout and the slab surface dried. Carpet can often be saved if it is clean water and dried quickly. We will tell you what can be saved after we inspect.

Can you help if the water is from outside?

Yes. Monsoon water and flooding need extra cleaning because they carry dirt and bacteria. See our flood damage cleanup page for more.
